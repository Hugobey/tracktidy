## What orange means

Serato's support page says lost files come from moving files that are already in your library. They show up as not found and are displayed in orange. The page talks about moving. A rename changes the path in the same way, so I treat a rename like a move. That part is my own reading, not Serato's wording. Source: Serato's [Relocate Lost Files](https://support.serato.com/hc/en-us/articles/14359604291599-Relocate-Lost-Files) page.

## Two ways to relocate

Both live in the Files panel.

1. **Relocate Lost Files.** Click the button. Serato scans your whole computer and any attached drives, then updates the library with the new locations. On a big library this can take a very long time.
2. **Relocate from folder.** Click it, go to the folder where you think the tracks are now, press Return, and Serato starts the search there.

I start with the second one. If I know where the files went, it saves time. If I do not, the first one is the safety net. Plug in any external drive before you start, since attached drives are part of the search.

## The iTunes warning

Before you relocate, turn off Show iTunes Library under Setup, then Library + Display. Serato's page warns that skipping this may cause duplicate entries to appear after relocation. Turn it back on when the search has finished.

Two entries for one track is its own kind of missing file, because the one with your cue points may be the one you cannot find.

## Stop, it's happening again

Three rules, and they are all about order.

- **One permanent music folder.** Not Downloads. Not Desktop. The place a file lands is the place it stays.
- **Finish the file before it is added.** Tags, BPM, key, and the name. The tag pass is in [how to edit MP3 metadata online](/guides/edit-mp3-metadata-online), and the naming pass is in [how to batch rename MP3 files online](/guides/batch-rename-mp3-files-online).
- **Do not tidy in Finder afterwards.** A quick rename is how one orange track becomes forty.

A name that carries the numbers is easier to spot in a crate when something goes wrong.

```
Before: 04-track-04.mp3
After:  Fred again.. - Delilah (pull me out of this) (134 BPM) [6A].mp3
```

The figures for that record are the ones on Mixgraph's [Delilah (pull me out of this)](https://www.mixgraph.io/tracks/fred-again-delilah-pull-me-out-of-this) page. If a BPM looks like half of what you expect, see [how to find the BPM of a song online](/guides/find-bpm-of-a-song-online). For the key, use the [Camelot wheel guide](/guides/camelot-wheel-harmonic-mixing).

## Quick answers

### Why are my Serato tracks orange?

Serato cannot find the file where it expects it. Serato's support page says the usual cause is moving files that are already in the library.

### How do I fix missing files in Serato DJ Pro?

Open the Files panel and use Relocate Lost Files, or Relocate from folder if you know where the tracks are now.

### Why did relocating create duplicate tracks?

Serato's page says leaving Show iTunes Library on can cause duplicates after relocation. Turn it off first and back on afterwards.
