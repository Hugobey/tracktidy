Your software says 10B. Your head says D major. On a small screen at two in the morning, you want the first one.

The Camelot wheel renames all 24 musical keys as a number from 1 to 12 and a letter, A or B. The point is that keys that blend well end up next to each other, so compatibility becomes counting. Here is the whole system, with real records.

## How to read the wheel

<figure>
  <img src="/images/guides/camelot-wheel.svg" alt="The Camelot wheel: an outer ring of major keys 1B to 12B and an inner ring of minor keys 1A to 12A." width="900" height="900" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>The 24 Camelot codes. B is the outer ring (major), A is the inner ring (minor). Original TrackTidy graphic.</figcaption>
</figure>

Four rules cover it.

- The number is the position around the wheel, 1 to 12.
- The letter is the mode. A is minor, B is major.
- Same number, other letter, is the relative major or minor. 8A is A minor, 8B is C major.
- One step up or down in number, same letter, is a neighbouring key.

From any code you have four safe moves. Starting on 10B (D major):

| Move | Lands on | How it feels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stay put | 10B | Same harmony, nothing to hear |
| Swap the letter | 10A | Same notes, darker mood |
| One step up | 11B | A small lift |
| One step down | 9B | A small settle |

Anything further away is not forbidden. It just asks more of your ear.

## Real keys, real records

These are the keys public track databases list for records most people know. Your copy can differ, since edits and remasters shift things.

| Track | Artist | BPM | Key | Camelot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One More Time | Daft Punk | 123 | D major | 10B |
| Wake Me Up | Avicii | 124 | D major | 10B |
| Get Lucky | Daft Punk, Pharrell Williams, Nile Rodgers | 116 | B minor | 10A |
| Levels | Avicii | 126 | E major | 12B |

Walk through it.

- **One More Time into Wake Me Up.** Same code, one BPM apart. This is the easy one.
- **One More Time into Get Lucky.** 10B to 10A is the relative minor, so the key works. The gap is 7 BPM, so you pitch it, or you pick your moment.
- **Wake Me Up into Levels.** 10B to 12B is two steps around the wheel. Tempo fits (124 to 126). The key is the question, so keep the blend short or lean on the drums.

The figures come from the [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/tracks/daft-punk-one-more-time) catalog pages for [One More Time](https://www.mixgraph.io/tracks/daft-punk-one-more-time), [Levels](https://www.mixgraph.io/tracks/avicii-levels-original-version), and the [Daft Punk](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/daft-punk) and [Avicii](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/avicii) artist pages. ## Key detection is an estimate

Even the people who made a record will not always give you a straight answer. Disclosure's Guy Lawrence has declined to say definitively what key Latch is in. The pianist Jacob Collier argued for F minor in a text message to the duo, and a DJ promo database lists it as F minor too. Source: the [Latch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latch_(song)) article on Wikipedia.

Analyzers disagree as well. Public [AcousticBrainz](https://acousticbrainz.org/60fa767a-d85d-4991-82bc-4294e0b11ae7) analyses of different versions of One More Time return F minor, G major, and D major.

So treat any key reading as a starting point. Play the two records. If the blend sounds sour, it is sour, whatever the number says. Your ear wins an argument with a key finder.

The key lives in the initial key frame, TKEY, one of the six fields worth fixing on a normal prep night. Pick one system, Camelot or musical key, and use it on every file. ## Quick answers

### What does 8A mean?

A minor. The number is the position on the wheel, and A means minor. 8B is the relative major, C major.

### Is the Camelot wheel the same as the circle of fifths?

Same neighbourhood logic, different labels. Neighbouring keys on the circle of fifths are neighbouring numbers on the Camelot wheel. It is a relabelling made for DJs.

### Can I mix tracks that are not on neighbouring keys?

Yes. Keep the blend short, use drums or effects, or pick records with little harmony. Your ear makes the call.
