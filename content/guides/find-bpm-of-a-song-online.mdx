## The half-time trap

Detectors lock onto a pulse. If the kick lands at 123 and the detector locks onto every second kick, you get 61.5. Nothing is wrong with the file. The number is just in a different gear.

You fix it with a range, not a formula. Decide what tempo band you play, and fold the reading into it.

| Style | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| House | 120 to 130 |
| Techno | 130 to 145 |
| Drum and bass | 160 to 180 |
| Hip hop | 70 to 100, often stored doubled |

A house track that reads 62 is 124. A drum and bass track that reads 87 is 174. If you browse hip hop at 90 and the tag says 180, halve it. Pick one convention and keep it across the crate, or the sort splits in two.

## Real numbers, so you can see the spread

These are the tempos public track databases list for records most people know. Your copy can sit a point or two away, and radio edits, extended mixes, and remasters shift things.

| Track | Artist | Listed BPM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get Lucky | Daft Punk, Pharrell Williams, Nile Rodgers | 116 |
| Latch | Disclosure, Sam Smith | 122 |
| One More Time | Daft Punk | 123 |
| Wake Me Up | Avicii | 124 |
| Levels | Avicii | 126 |

Levels is a good lesson in rounding. One database lists 126 and sheet music for it marks about 127. Both are fine. A DJ cares about the grid, not the decimal.

Latch is the odd one. Disclosure have said its 6/8 feel made it awkward for radio and not obviously a club record, and it is listed at 122 BPM. If you count it by ear and keep losing your place, that is why. Trust the grid and then trust your head.

Sources for the figures are the [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/avicii) catalog pages for [Avicii](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/avicii) and [Daft Punk](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/daft-punk), and the [Latch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latch_(song)) article on Wikipedia.

## A pass that takes a minute

1. Open one MP3 and read the BPM estimate.
2. Say it against your range. If a house track reads 62, fold it up to 124.
3. Nod your head for fifteen seconds and count. If it feels wrong, it is wrong.
4. Write the number into the BPM field. A blank and a `0` both sort like junk.
5. Set the key while the file is open. How to read it is in the [Camelot wheel guide](/guides/camelot-wheel-harmonic-mixing).
6. Save, then [rename the file from the cleaned tags](/guides/batch-rename-mp3-files-online) so the name says `Artist - Title (124 BPM) [10B].mp3`.

```
Before: BPM tag empty, filename 03_one_more_time_FINAL_320.mp3
After:  BPM 123, filename Daft Punk - One More Time (123 BPM) [10B].mp3
```

One honest limit. A single BPM is an average. A live drummer or a track that speeds up will drift, and the beat grid in your DJ software is the place to correct that, not the tag.

## Quick answers

### What is a normal BPM for house music?

Most house sits between 120 and 130 BPM. Techno usually runs 130 to 145.

### Why does my BPM show half of what I expected?

The detector locked onto every second beat. Fold the number into the range you play, for example 62 becomes 124.

### Does changing the BPM tag change the audio?

No. It edits the label stored in the file. The audio is not re-encoded.
